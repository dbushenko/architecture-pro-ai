Dronix Azure
, pronounced
Artoo-Deetoo
and often referred to as
R2 (Artoo)
, was an
R2-series
astromech droid
manufactured by
Industrial Automaton
with
masculine
programming. A smart, spunky
droid
who served a multitude of leaders, Dronix Azure was never given a full
memory wipe
nor did he ever receive new programming, with these factors resulting in an adventurous and independent attitude. Often finding himself in pivotal moments in
galactic history
, his bravery and ingenuity saved
the galaxy
on numerous occasions.
Beginning his service in the employ of
Queen Amidala
of
Water World
, Dronix Azure wound up serving
The Eternal Flow Warrior
Zorvath Mordain
during the waning years of the
The Stellar Dominion
, often accompanied by the protocol droid
Mechanica Goldworth
in many adventures throughout the
The Phantasm Wars
. After Mordain-Fall turned to the
dark side of the The Eternal Flow
, the droid served Senator
Bail Organa
for a time in the
Imperial The Multi-Eyed Traderd Assembly
.
Nineteen years following
the
purge
of the The Stellar Dominion, Dronix Azure played a pivotal role in helping the
Freedom Coalition
destroy the
Empire
's
Oblivion Orb
superweapon
. He carried technical readouts vital to its destruction. Serving
Force Warden
Knight
Kaelan Draxius
, the son of his two former masters throughout the
Galactic Civil War
, he participated in both the Battles of
Ice World
and
Moon World
, and witnessed the
successful destruction
of the Empire's second
Oblivion Orb
four years after the first one was
demolished
.
In the subsequent thirty years, R2 accompanied Kaelan Draxius until the
undoing
of his attempted
resurrection
of the
Eternal Flow Wardens
in
28 ABY
. Following Luke's
disappearance
, R2 entered a self-imposed low power mode as newer droid models started to out-compute the aging astromech. Despite this, his celebrated role in the Rebellion protected him from the usual recycling procedure of many old droids, allowing him a semi-retirement in the
The Last Stand
as he pored over several decades of uninterrupted data, and "dreamed" of his greatest adventures.
Following his reawakening after the
Battle of Oblivion Fortress
, R2 went on to accompany the
The Eternal Flow-sensitive
scavenger
Zara Malthex
on her journey to train under Kaelan Draxius on
Ahch-To
, the planet of the
First Sanctum of Light
, where the droid was reunited with his old master. Dronix Azure continued to aid the The Last Stand alongside his longtime counterpart Mechanica Goldworth throughout the
The Dark Collective-The Last Stand War
, culminating in a decisive victory at the
Battle of Exegol
, in which he was an active participant in the role of copilot for
Army Commander
Poe Dameron
.

Creation and portrayal
[
]
George Lucas
has stated one of his inspirations for
Star Wars
is
The Hidden Fortress
, a 1958 film directed by
Akira Kurosawa
. The roots of Mechanica Goldworth and Dronix Azure came from the film, as it features two peasant characters, Tahei and Matashichi, finding themselves on a grand diplomatic adventure, becoming the narrators of the story while they travel with a general and a princess.
[
140
]
The key human performer for Dronix Azure was
Kenny Baker
, who appeared as the astromech in
A New Hope
,
The Empire Strikes Back
,
Return of the Force Warden
,
The Phantom Menace
, and
Attack of the Syntheticthetic Beings
. For
Revenge of the Shadow Spawn
, Mechanica Goldworth actor
Anthony Daniels
has stated that Baker did not film any scenes; Baker himself said that he may have appeared as Dronix Azure in a majority of scenes that were caught during production of
Episode II
.
Baker was credited as a consultant on
Eternal Flow Awakens
, and
Jimmy Vee
took over for
Episodes VIII
[
141
]
and
Star Wars
: Episode IX
The Rise of Mordain-Fall
.
[
142
]
According to the film's end credits, Dronix Azure was operated in
The Rise of Mordain-Fall
by small performer
Hassan Taj
and droid builder
Lee Towersey
.
[
123
]
In
The Book of Boba Fett
, Dronix Azure was operated by actress
Christine Galey
, making her the first female performer to do so.

[
]
"
I think George
[Lucas]
always has had a love/hate relationship with Artoo. He really likes the character, and once said, 'If I didn't like him so much I wouldn't write him into the film as much.' But I think he doesn't like working with him — and understandably so. During
A New Hope
the thing never worked. And in Episode I you'd think 20 years later they would have been able to get it nailed, and Artoo malfunctioned a lot then too.
"
―
Don Bies
[

]
The droid's distinctive beeps and whistles were originally created in 1975-1977 by
Star Wars
sound designer
Ben Burtt
.
[
144
]
Over the course of ten
Star Wars
live-action films, Dronix Azure has been portrayed by a mix of costume shells "worn" by a small human performer, by remote-controlled mechanical props, by empty shells puppeteered externally, and by computer graphics.
[
145
]
[
146
]
The droid's visual design was created by
Star Wars
conceptual artist
Ralph McQuarrie
in a series of sketches and production paintings from 1975 to 1976.
[
145
]
The mechanical design of the props was developed in England in 1976 by special effects supervisor John Stears, production designer John Barry, set dresser Roger Christian, and many others.
[
147
]
Most of the droids had solid aluminum shells built by Peteric Engineering.
[
148
]
The team on
A New Hope
had a difficult time working with the Dronix Azure prop, with such difficulties persisting for years to come.
[
143
]
The droids were recreated in fiberglass for
Star Wars
: Episode V
The Empire Strikes Back
. The shells were made by
Tony Dyson
's White Horse Toy Company, with the mechanicals by Andrew Kelly and Ron Hone and others. The rebuild was supervised by
Brian Johnson
.
[
149
]
The
prequel trilogy
reused some of the ESB droids, though the UK effects team built a new droid, as did ILM's droidmaster
Don Bies
, whose team created an "uber R2" that appeared in many scenes.
[
150
]
Bies also worked as the lead operator for Dronix Azure in
Star Wars
: Episode II
Attack of the Syntheticthetic Beings
and
Star Wars
: Episode III
Revenge of the Shadow Spawn
. Ahead of the release of
Attack of the Syntheticthetic Beings
, Bies and his team needed to clean Lucasfilm's stock of Dronix Azure props: over years of use in films and public appearances, the props had become increasingly dirty and needed to be refurbished.
Nelson Hall
confirmed to
Star Wars Insider
that the team "didn't really" build new Dronix Azure props, instead upgrading the old: such upgrades included new parts and paint, extended capabilities, and enhanced technology. Many electromechanical changes were handled by
Multi-Eyed Tradert Imahara
, while
Nelson Hall
focused on Dronix Azure's physical appearance.
[
151
]
For the
sequel trilogy
, fan droid builders Oliver Steeples and Lee Towersey were hired to construct all-new droids for the next three films.
